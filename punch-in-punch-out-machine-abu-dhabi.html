// THE BASICS

What an Employee Punching Machine Actually Does

A punch-in machine and a punch-out machine are the same device doing two jobs: it identifies the employee, stamps the date and time, and stores that event as a punch. Whether people call it an attendance punching machine, a biometric punching machine or simply the punching system, the purpose is identical — a trustworthy record of who was at work and when, without paper registers or handwritten timesheets.

On its own, a list of punches is not much use. The value comes from the punch-in/punch-out system around the device: software that pairs each IN with its OUT, applies shift and overtime rules and produces timesheets for HR. VOWTECH sets up both halves, from the biometric devices at the entrance through to a full attendance system installation when a complete setup is required.

Punching machines are also network equipment. They need power, a data point or reliable Wi-Fi, and a path to the PC, server or cloud service that holds the records. If the entrance has no network point, our networking and infrastructure engineers add one, and companies with several branches can bring every machine into one UAE-wide attendance system.

// TYPES OF PUNCHING MACHINE

Three Ways to Punch In and Out

Every punching machine answers one question — who is this? — before it records the time. The difference between models is how they answer it.

01

Fingerprint Punching Machine

The employee places a finger on the sensor and the machine matches it against the enrolled template. It is the familiar choice for offices, shops and clinics, and it cannot be lent to a colleague the way a card can.

No cards or PINs to manage
Compact and simple to use
A second finger can be enrolled as backup
Less suited to worn or dirty hands
02

Face Recognition Punching Machine

The terminal recognises the employee as they look at the screen — nothing to touch. It copes well with shift changes where many people arrive together, and with workshops or kitchens where hands are wet, gloved or dusty.

Contactless punch in and punch out
Fast at busy shift changes
Needs sensible lighting and mounting height
More on face recognition systems
03

Card & PIN Punching Machine

Staff tap a proximity card or key in a PIN. It is straightforward and suits visitors, contractors and short-term workers, though cards and PINs can be shared, so many employers combine them with a biometric check.

Easy for temporary staff
Cards can double as door access
PIN useful as a fallback method
Often paired with fingerprint or face

// FROM PUNCH TO TIMESHEET

How a Punch Becomes a Timesheet

The journey of a single attendance record, from the moment a finger touches the sensor to the figure payroll receives.

01

Employee Punches

The person presents a finger, face or card; the machine confirms who they are and shows a success message.

02

Device Stores the Event

Employee number, date, time and device are saved in the machine memory, even if the network is down.

03

Records Sync

Punches are sent over LAN, Wi-Fi or the internet to the attendance software and added to the database.

04

Rules Are Applied

The software pairs each IN with an OUT, matches them to the shift and calculates hours, lateness and overtime.

05

Timesheet Produced

HR reviews exceptions such as missed punches, approves the period and exports the figures for payroll.

// FAQ

Punching Machine Questions

What is a punch-in/punch-out machine?

It is a device, usually mounted near an entrance, that records when an employee starts work (punch in) and finishes (punch out). It identifies the person by fingerprint, face, card or PIN and saves the time against their employee number. Attendance software then turns those punches into working hours. Our guide what is a punch-in/punch-out system goes into more detail.

Which is better, a fingerprint or a face recognition punching machine?

Neither is better everywhere. Fingerprint punching machines are compact and well suited to offices and shops. Face recognition is contactless and handles crowds and difficult hand conditions more comfortably, which helps in warehouses, kitchens and workshops. Read our comparison of fingerprint and face recognition attendance before deciding.

What happens if an employee forgets to punch out?

The software flags the day as an exception because it has an IN without a matching OUT. An authorised HR user or supervisor can then add a manual correction with a reason, and the adjustment is normally logged so there is a record of who changed what. Nothing is silently guessed.

Does a punching machine work if the network or internet is down?

In most cases, yes. Current machines keep punches in internal memory and upload them once the connection is restored, so staff can carry on punching. The machine does need power, so for entrances prone to power cuts it is worth considering a model with a backup battery or a small UPS.

Can data from the punching machine be used for payroll?

Yes. Attendance software can produce monthly summaries of hours worked, lateness, absence and overtime, and export them as a file for your payroll process. Direct integration with HR or payroll software is possible with some packages; we check what yours supports before promising a link.

Where should a punching machine be installed?

Close to the entrance staff use every day, indoors or under cover, at a comfortable height, with power and a network point or strong Wi-Fi nearby. Face recognition terminals should not face direct sunlight. Larger premises usually need a machine at each staff entrance to avoid queues at shift change.
