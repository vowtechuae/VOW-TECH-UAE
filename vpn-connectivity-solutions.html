// OVERVIEW

Private Links Between Your Sites, Carried Over the Public Internet

A VPN creates an encrypted tunnel across the internet so that traffic between two points cannot be read by anyone in between. For a business this solves two everyday problems: branch offices that need to share servers, files and applications as though they were on one network, and employees who need those same resources when they are away from the office.

VOWTECH plans the connectivity before touching any configuration: which sites must talk to each other, which users need remote access, what they should reach and what they should not. Tunnels normally terminate on your firewall or a dedicated VPN appliance, so the design also covers internet links, IP addressing and capacity at each location.

A VPN is only useful while it stays stable. Once the links are live they can be watched and maintained through our VPN and network monitoring service, so a dropped tunnel or an expiring certificate is noticed and dealt with before staff start reporting that the shared drive has disappeared.

// VPN TYPES

Three Ways to Connect Securely

Most organisations end up using more than one of these. The design work is deciding which traffic belongs on which.

01

Site-to-Site VPN

A permanent tunnel between the firewalls at two or more locations. Staff do nothing — the head office, branch, warehouse or site cabin simply behave like parts of one network.

Always-on links between locations
No software on user devices
Hub-and-spoke or meshed layouts
Suits shared servers, ERP and CCTV viewing
02

Remote Access VPN

A client on the laptop or phone builds a tunnel to the office on demand. Each user signs in individually, so access can be granted, limited and removed person by person.

For home and travelling staff
Per-user sign-in and permissions
Works with multi-factor authentication
Access ends when the account is disabled
03

Cloud & Hybrid VPN

A tunnel from your office network to a private cloud or hosted environment, so servers in the cloud are reached over private addressing rather than being published to the internet.

Office-to-cloud private connectivity
Keeps hosted servers off the public web
Useful during phased migrations
See our private cloud services

// FAQ

VPN Connectivity Questions

What is the difference between a site-to-site VPN and a remote access VPN?

A site-to-site VPN joins two networks permanently, firewall to firewall, and users are not aware of it. A remote access VPN connects one device to the office when the user signs in through a VPN client. Offices with branches and mobile staff typically use both, each with its own access rules.

Do we need special equipment at every office?

Each site needs a device that can terminate a VPN tunnel — usually a business-grade firewall or router, not the basic unit supplied with an internet line. We check what you already have before recommending anything new. Options are described on our VPN appliances page.

Will a VPN slow down our applications?

Encryption adds a small overhead, but the main factors are the internet links at each end and the capacity of the devices doing the encryption. During the design we look at line speeds, the applications that will cross the tunnel and the firewall throughput, so that the limits are known before go-live.

Is a VPN enough to secure remote working?

It protects the connection, not the device. A laptop with malware can still carry it through the tunnel. We normally pair remote access with multi-factor sign-in, limited access rules and endpoint security on the devices that connect, so that one weak laptop does not expose the office.
